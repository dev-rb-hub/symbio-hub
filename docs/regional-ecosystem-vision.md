# Regional Ecosystem Vision

Where Symbio Hub could go beyond a single marketplace. This page states **hypotheses and intent**, not commitments, partnerships, outcomes or advice. No organisation named or described generically here has agreed to take part. Maturity labels are defined in the [roadmap](ROADMAP.md).

## The idea

Technology investment in regional Australia (new data centres, cloud regions, government digital programs) creates demand for local skills, but demand alone does not guarantee that local people and businesses capture the benefit. Work, wages and intellectual property can still flow to metropolitan or offshore providers.

Symbio Hub explores a simple loop: **learn, certify, deliver locally, reinvest locally**. A marketplace with verified identity, milestone escrow and recorded delivery evidence gives regional experts a safe, auditable way to turn skills into paid local work, and gives regional SMEs a trusted way to buy it.

## Possibilities to explore

### 1. Community edition as a learning platform (**Planned**)
- Students and early-career experts work on small, scoped, supervised projects with real delivery evidence.
- Verified evidence builds a portable track record, a stepping stone toward certification and employment.
- Universities, TAFEs and innovation hubs could run cohorts on a self-hosted Community edition at no licence cost.

### 2. Local talent retention for councils and regional bodies (**Planned**)
- Many regions worry about talent drain to capital cities. A local pipeline from study to paid local work is one possible countermeasure.
- Optional regional impact reporting (jobs, projects, spend kept in region), designed to minimise personal data collected. Pro edition candidate.

### 3. Data-centre and infrastructure investment (**Planned**, exploratory)
- Where large technology providers or governments invest in regional infrastructure, a local services marketplace could help route skilled work to local businesses.
- Horizon 3 in the [roadmap](ROADMAP.md) mentions optional allocation tracking for sponsored compute. This is an idea to test with partners, not an arrangement.

### 4. Socio-economic responsibility of institutions (exploratory)
- Financial services, government and learning institutions each have an interest in building regional capability: training, certification, local business formation, and local lending or investment.
- Symbio Hub could provide the **transparent delivery record** that such programs need to show where funding went and what was delivered.
- Any sponsorship or program participation would be separate, written, and clearly distinguished from the open-source project.

### 5. A pattern others can copy (**Planned**)
- The core is provider-neutral and open source. Another region or country could swap payment, identity and hosting adapters and run the same model under local rules ([architecture overview](architecture/overview.md)).
- Documentation, ADRs and the Community edition are the reusable assets.

## What we are careful not to claim

- That Symbio Hub reduces inflation or guarantees economic outcomes. A larger share of spend staying in a region may improve resilience; that is a hypothesis to measure in a pilot, not a result.
- That any bank, government body, university, council or technology company is a customer, partner or sponsor, unless it is stated in writing in a linked source.
- That the platform is certified or compliant with any standard. See the [compliance posture](compliance/README.md).
- That investment programs, grants or sponsored compute are available through Symbio Hub today.

## How we would test it

1. Run a small regional pilot with a handful of SMEs and local experts on sandbox payments (**Planned**).
2. Run one supervised graduate or student cohort with an innovation hub or university, if one agrees to take part (**Planned**).
3. Publish what we learn, including what did not work.
4. Only then discuss Pro and Enterprise editions with design partners.
